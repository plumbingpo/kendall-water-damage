How Structural Drying Works in Kendall, FL

Home / Blog / Understanding Professional Structural Drying in Kendall, FL

Ask people around Kendall, FL what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. After extraction removes standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in building materials: drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, and insulation. Mold, lingering smells, and deteriorating materials are the result when the trapped water is not taken out correctly. Here we cover how professional drying works and the reasons it outperforms a do-it-yourself effort by a wide margin.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

The field behind professional drying is called psychrometry, which examines how mixtures of gas and vapor behave physically and thermodynamically. For a drying job, that comes down to knowing how relative humidity, airflow, and temperature combine to move moisture out of a wet structure. With psychrometric calculations, an IICRC-certified technician works out the setup for a given job: how many dehumidifiers and which type, where the air movers go, and what temperature and humidity to aim for.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

Consumer dehumidifiers and fans do not compare to the machines a restoration crew brings. LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers can remove 15 to 30 or more pints of moisture per hour from the air, compared to 2 to 3 pints per hour for a residential unit. Evaporation is sped up by air movers that deliver a directed, high-velocity stream of 2,500 CFM or more across wet surfaces. Desiccant dehumidifiers are used for specialized situations, including cold-weather drying and extreme moisture loads. This equipment represents tens of thousands of dollars in investment and requires specific training to deploy effectively.

Each Stage of Professional Drying

There is a defined method behind every structural drying project. First, a thorough moisture assessment maps all affected areas using moisture meters and thermal imaging. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. Next, psychrometric calculations decide the position of each air mover and dehumidifier so that water evaporates and is removed efficiently. Sometimes the space is heated so evaporation goes faster. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. Equipment stays until readings for all affected materials come acceptably close to dry reference standards.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

Expect 3 to 5 days for the majority of residential jobs when proper commercial gear is used. How long it takes comes down to how much moisture there is, which materials got wet (drywall dries far faster than concrete), how the building is constructed, the ambient conditions, and how many machines of what type are running. As a rule, standard drywall needs 2 to 3 days, wood framing 3 to 4 days, and concrete 5 to 7 days or more. You will get a projected finish date from the initial readings, and it is revised as the structure dries.

Why Monitoring Matters

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. Surfaces can feel dry to the touch while holding dangerous moisture levels deep within the material. Verifying it takes a moisture meter that has been calibrated. The drying records show your insurer that industry protocols were followed correctly, which supports the claim and helps protect what the property is worth.
