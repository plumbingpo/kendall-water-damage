Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Kendall: What to Know

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Do not think of it as clean water. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Do not wait for the water to drain away by itself.
Insurance

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • A power outage during the storm with no battery backup on the pump.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.

What Happens if You Wait

Basement Floor Coverings

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Outlets and Wiring

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Our Process

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Pump Out the Basement

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.
